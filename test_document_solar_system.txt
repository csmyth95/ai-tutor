The Solar System: An Overview
The Solar System consists of the Sun and all the objects that orbit 
around it, including eight planets, their moons, dwarf planets, 
asteroids, and comets. The Sun, a medium-sized star, contains 99.86% 
of the Solar System's mass and provides the gravitational force that 
holds everything together.
The four inner planets - Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars - are 
called terrestrial planets because they have solid, rocky surfaces. 
Mercury is the smallest and closest to the Sun, while Venus is the 
hottest due to its thick atmosphere trapping heat. Earth is the only 
planet known to support life, thanks to its liquid water and 
protective atmosphere. Mars, the "Red Planet," has the largest 
volcano in the Solar System, Olympus Mons.
The four outer planets - Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune - are 
called gas giants (or ice giants for Uranus and Neptune). Jupiter is 
the largest planet, with a mass greater than all other planets 
combined. Saturn is famous for its spectacular ring system made of 
ice and rock particles. Uranus rotates on its side, likely due to a 
massive collision in its past. Neptune, the farthest planet, has the 
strongest winds in the Solar System.
Beyond Neptune lies the Kuiper Belt, home to dwarf planets like 
Pluto, which was reclassified from planet status in 2006. The Solar 
System formed approximately 4.6 billion years ago from a giant cloud 
of gas and dust called a solar nebula.
